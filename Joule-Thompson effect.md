Joule-Thompson effect relates the changes in internal energy to a compression or expansion of gas. The Joule-Thompson is an adiabatic process (Change in enthalpy is zero). It can be explained as a change in Pressure and Volume (as flowing through an orifice) can result in either a rise in internal energy (Temp + State) or a lowering of internal energy based on initial conditions and nature of gas. 
$$H = U + PV$$
where H is enthalpy and U is internal energy. 
If the rate of PV increases during the process it will result in a lower U, resulting in cooling during expansion or having a positive jules-thompson coefficient. Likewise if it warms during expansion it will have a negative jules-thompson coefficient.
When fluids go from a negative to a positive (at low temps) or a positive to a negative (at high temps) it is known as temperature inversion. All gases have a positive jules-thompson coefficient at room temp (25C) except HYDROGEN, HELIUM, and NEON.
